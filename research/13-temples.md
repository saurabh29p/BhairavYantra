# 13 · Main Temples / प्रमुख मंदिर

*Last updated: 2026-11-15. Grades follow [00-methodology-and-sources.md](../docs/01-methodology-and-sources.md).*

**How to read this file / इस फ़ाइल को कैसे पढ़ें**

- **History** means a date or fact stated by an inscription, a government source, or a published source, as cited. **Legend** means the temple's sacred story. The two are always kept apart.
  *"इतिहास" वह है जो शिलालेख, सरकारी स्रोत या प्रकाशित स्रोत में दर्ज है; "कथा" मंदिर की पवित्र परंपरा है। दोनों को हमेशा अलग रखा गया है।*
- Most entries rely on Wikipedia articles **and the references they cite**. Where we read only the Wikipedia text and not the reference it cites, the grade is **C**. Where we read an official or government page directly, that is stated.
- **Images:** no image licence has been verified yet. Do not use a photo on the website until its Wikimedia Commons file page and licence have been checked. Section 4 lists where to look.

## 1. Bhairava temples / भैरव मंदिर

### 1.1 Kāla Bhairava, Vārāṇasī (Kāśī) / काल भैरव, वाराणसी
- **Location:** Visheshwarganj (Bhaironath), Vārāṇasī, Uttar Pradesh.
- **Deity form:** silver face; only the face is visible and the rest of the image is covered with cloth; seated on a dog, holding a trident. *(Wikipedia; grade C)*
- **History:** "Exact date of construction … is not known but it is estimated that the current structure was built in the mid 17th century AD." Wikipedia cites varanasi.org.in and two tourism portals, which we have not read. *(grade C ⚠)*
- **Textual basis:** Skanda Purāṇa, Kāśīkhaṇḍa, Pūrvārdha 31.46 gives Kālabhairava lordship (*ādhipatya*) over Kāśī. Śiva Purāṇa, Śatarudra Saṃhitā 8.50 has the same verse. *(grade A‑w; see [file 01](01-origin-of-bhairava-puranas.md))*
- **Popular belief:** that he is the "Kotwāl" (police chief) of Kāśī. Wikipedia marks this "as per popular belief". The Purāṇic verse grants lordship, but the word "kotwāl" is not in it. *(grade D)*
- Source: [Wikipedia, Kaal Bhairav Mandir, Varanasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaal_Bhairav_Mandir,_Varanasi)

### 1.2 Kāla Bhairava, Ujjain (Bhairavgarh) / काल भैरव, उज्जैन
- **Location:** Bhairavgarh, Ujjain, Madhya Pradesh, on the Kṣiprā (Shipra) river.
- **Deity form:** a face in rock covered with vermilion, with a silver head wearing a Maratha-style turban. *(grade C)*
- **History:** the present structure was built over the remains of an older temple. Wikipedia cites the [Ujjain District Collector's temples page (archived)](https://web.archive.org/web/20150921131905/http://www.ujjain.nic.in/TouristInfo/temples/temples.html). *(grade C)*
- **Tradition:** the original temple was built by King Bhadrasena, and the temple is mentioned in the Avantī Khaṇḍa of the Skanda Purāṇa. The Avantī Khaṇḍa reference has **not yet been checked** in the text. ⚠ Attributing the rebuilding to the Marathas is local tradition only. *(grade D)*
- **Observance:** liquor is offered to the deity. This is reported by India Today (1994) and the Times of India (2006). *(grade C)*
- Source: [Wikipedia, Kal Bhairav temple, Ujjain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kal_Bhairav_temple,_Ujjain)

### 1.3 Not yet researched / अभी शोध बाकी
Baṭuka Bhairava (Kamachha, Vārāṇasī), Kāla Bhairava (Adhiyamankottai, Dharmapuri), Kāla Bhairava (Hanuman Dhoka, Kathmandu), Bhairavnāth (Kedārnāth), Bhairon temple (Vaiṣṇo Devī), the eight Bhairavas of Kāśī, and Svarṇākarṣaṇa Bhairava temples. We found **no source** yet that names the eight Bhairava shrines of Kāśī.

## 2. Kālī temples / काली मंदिर

### 2.1 Kālīghāṭ, Kolkata / कालीघाट, कोलकाता
- **Deity:** Kālī in black touchstone, with three eyes, a golden tongue and four hands. The temple's own history page says the image was made by "two saints – Brahmananda Giri and Atmaram Giri". *(official page; grade C)*
- **History (official page):** "The Kalighat temple in its present form is only about 200 years old." It says a small temple was built by King Mānasiṃha in the early sixteenth century, and that the present temple "was completed in 1809". *(read directly)*
- **History (Wikipedia):** construction began in 1798 under Santosh Roy Chowdhury and took 11 years. Wikipedia notes that this family's role "is disputed". It also says the site is referred to in the 15th-century *Mānasā Bhāsān*. *(grade C ⚠)*
- **Legend:** a toe of Satī's right foot fell here (Śākta Pīṭha). Wikipedia attributes this to the Devī Bhāgavata, the Kālikā Purāṇa and the Śakti Pīṭha Stotram. Not yet checked in those texts. *(grade C ⚠)*
- Sources: [Official history page](https://kalighatkalitemple.com/article/id/230/history) · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalighat_Kali_Temple)

### 2.2 Dakṣiṇeśvar, Kolkata / दक्षिणेश्वर, कोलकाता
- **Deity:** Bhavatāriṇī Kālī, standing on Śiva on a silver thousand-petalled lotus.
- **History:** founded by Rāṇī Rāsmaṇi. Built 1847–1855; the image was installed on Snāna Yātrā day, **31 May 1855**. Wikipedia cites Mehrotra (2008). *(grade C)*
- **Legend:** Rāṇī Rāsmaṇi's dream ("There is no need to go to Banaras"). Wikipedia marks this "according to traditional accounts". *(grade D)*
- **⚠ Warning:** Wikipedia lists `dakshineswarkalitemple.org` as the official website, but that domain now serves an unrelated spam page. **Do not link it.**
- Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakshineswar_Kali_Temple)

### 2.3 Kālkājī, Delhi / कालकाजी, दिल्ली
- **History:** "The core structure was built no earlier than 1764 AD" (Wikipedia, citing Cooper 1865 and India's National Mission on Monuments and Antiquities). It was enlarged by Durga Singh around 1783, and a dome was funded in 1816 by Mirza Raja Kidarnath. *(grade C)*
- **Legend:** Kālikā Devī was born at this site. Wikipedia cites *A Gazetteer of Delhi* (1912). *(grade C)*
- Official site (per Wikipedia, not opened): shrikalkajimandir.in
- Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalkaji_Mandir)

### 2.4 Pāvāgaḍh Kālikā Mātā, Gujarat / पावागढ़ कालिका माता
- **History:** Wikipedia says the Jain Acalagaccha was founded here in 1113 CE, and that the Mahākālī image was installed by Jain lay followers. It cites Jain texts on jainqq.org. ⚠ This is a contested claim, and the Hindu temple tradition differs. *(grade C ⚠)*
- Source: [Wikipedia, Pavagadh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavagadh)

### 2.5 Not yet researched
Gaḍhkālikā, Ujjain.

## 3. Mahāvidyā temples / महाविद्या मंदिर

**Scholarly note:** Wikipedia's Dhūmāvatī article cites Kinsley (1988, p. 175) saying the idea of ten Mahāvidyās "may not be earlier than the 12th century". *(grade C)*

### 3.1 Kāmākhyā temple complex, Guwahati, Assam (all ten) / कामाख्या मंदिर परिसर
- **Deity:** Kāmākhyā, worshipped as a yoni-shaped hollow in the rock that is fed by a spring.
- **History:** the temple was rebuilt in 1565, under Chilarai in the reign of Koch king Nara Narayan, according to an inscription. Wikipedia cites Sarkar (1992). It was later rebuilt again under the Ahoms. *(grade C)*
- **The ten Mahāvidyās at Kāmākhyā:** Wikipedia lists all ten, citing Jae-Eun Shin, "Yoni, Yoginis and Mahavidyas", *Studies in History* 26(1), 2010, p. 4. Tripurasundarī, Mātaṅgī and Kamalā are inside the main temple, and the other seven have their own temples. *(grade C)*
  - Locations on the hill, according to the private site [kamakhya.org](https://kamakhya.org/dashamahavidyas-of-kamakhya/) *(grade D)*: Kālī, Tārā and Chinnamastā beside the stairway; Tripurasundarī, Mātaṅgī and Kamalā in the garbhagṛha; Bhuvaneśvarī on the east of the hill; Bhairavī on the south; Dhūmāvatī on the southern slope; Bagalāmukhī on the south-east.
  - kamakhya.org claims this is "the only site in the world" with temples of all ten. That is unsourced, and §3.4 shows Rajrappa also has all ten. ⚠
- **Legend:** in the Kālikā Purāṇa, this is where Satī's yoni fell. Wikipedia notes this is "not corroborated in the Devi Bhagavata". *(grade C ⚠)*
- **Administration:** in July 2015 the Supreme Court transferred administration to the Bordeuri Samaj (Wikipedia, citing the Indian Express). *(grade C)*
- **Official site:** [maakamakhya.org](https://www.maakamakhya.org/) (confirmed). kamakhya.org is a private site.
- Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamakhya_Temple)

### 3.2 Tārā: Tārāpīṭh, Birbhum, West Bengal / तारापीठ
- **Deity:** a stone image of Tārā suckling Śiva, covered by a metal image.
- **History:** no founding date is documented. The saint Bāmākhepā (1837–1911) is associated with the site (Wikipedia, citing Kinsley 1997, p. 111). *(grade C)*
- **Legend:** the third eye of Satī fell here. The sage Vasiṣṭha worshipped Tārā here. A further oral legend has the Buddha advising Vasiṣṭha (Kinsley 1997, pp. 106–109). *(grade C)*
- Official site (per Wikipedia, not opened): tarapith.in · Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarapith_Temple)

### 3.3 Tripurasundarī: Mātābāḍī, Udaipur, Tripura / त्रिपुरसुन्दरी, उदयपुर (त्रिपुरा)
- **History (Government of Tripura):** "Maharaja Dhanya Manikya founded the Tripura Sundari Temple in the year 1501." The same page says "The present temple is constructed during the reign of Maharaja Radhakishore Manikya Bahadur", which was much later. So 1501 is the founding date, not the date of the present building. ⚠ The page also says the original manuscripts "have been destroyed". *(read directly)*
- **Legend:** a dream told Dhanya Māṇikya to install the goddess on a hill that held a Viṣṇu temple. Satī's right foot fell here (the government page cites the "Pīṭhamālā Grantha"). *(grade C)*
- **Official site:** [tripurasundari.tripura.gov.in](https://tripurasundari.tripura.gov.in/history) · [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripura_Sundari_Temple)

### 3.4 Chinnamastā: Rajrappa, Ramgarh, Jharkhand / छिन्नमस्ता, रजरप्पा
- **Location:** at the confluence of the Dāmodar and Bherā (Bhairavī) rivers.
- **History:** no founding date. The site was "well-established as a centre of Chhinnamasta by the 18th century". *(Wikipedia; grade C)*
- **The complex** also has temples of Tārā, Ṣoḍaśī, Bhuvaneśvarī, Bhairavī, Bagalā, Kamalā, Mātaṅgī and Dhūmāvatī, and of Dakṣiṇa Kālī. *(grade C)*
- Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhinnamasta_Temple,_Rajrappa)

### 3.5 Chinnamastā (Chintpūrṇī), Una, Himachal Pradesh / चिंतपूर्णी
- **History:** the completion date is unknown. Paṇḍit Mai Dās "is generally believed to have established this shrine" about twelve generations ago. This is a traditional estimate. *(grade D)*
- Official site (per Wikipedia, not opened): chintpurni.in · Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chintpurni)

### 3.6 Bagalāmukhī and Dhūmāvatī: Pītāmbarā Pīṭh, Datia, Madhya Pradesh / पीताम्बरा पीठ, दतिया
- **History:** Wikipedia says it "was established by Shree Swami Ji in 1920s", citing an archived MP Tourism page. The Incredible India page gives a late-1920s arrival and an image installed in the mid-1930s. ⚠ The two dates conflict; to be checked with the Pīṭh itself.
- The Pīṭh has a separate **Dhūmāvatī** shrine (Wikipedia, Dhūmāvatī article, citing datia.nic.in). This is one of the very few temples of Dhūmāvatī. *(grade C)*
- Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitambara_Peeth) · [Incredible India (Govt. of India)](https://www.incredibleindia.gov.in/en/madhya-pradesh/datia/shri-pitambara-peeth)

### 3.7 Bagalāmukhī: Nalkheḍā, Agar Malwa, Madhya Pradesh / बगलामुखी, नलखेड़ा
- **History:** restored in 1815 (Wikipedia, citing Patrika, 2016). No founding date. *(grade C)*
- **Iconography:** a triad of Bagalāmukhī with Lakṣmī and Sarasvatī. *(grade C)*
- Official site (per Wikipedia, not opened): mabaglamukhi.org · Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagalamukhi_Temple,_Nalkheda)

### 3.8 Bagalāmukhī: Bankhaṇḍī, Kangra, Himachal Pradesh / बगलामुखी, बनखंडी
- **Legend only:** Rāma worshipped here before the war with Rāvaṇa, and the Pāṇḍavas built the temple in a single night. No founding date. *(grade D)*
- Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagalamukhi_Temple,_Bankhandi,_HP)

### 3.9 Dhūmāvatī: Vārāṇasī / धूमावती, वाराणसी
- A temple where Dhūmāvatī is the main deity. It claims to be a Śākta Pīṭha. No founding date. Wikipedia cites Kinsley 1997, p. 185. *(grade C)*
- Source: [Wikipedia, Dhumavati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhumavati)

### 3.10 Bhuvaneśvarī / भुवनेश्वरी
- Shrines named on Wikipedia: Bhuvanagiri (Siddapur, Uttara Kannada, Karnataka); Puducherry–Pudukkottai (Tamil Nadu, founded by Santhānanda Swāmī, no date); Gondal (Gujarat, "established in 1946"); and Kāmākhyā. *(grade C)*
- Source: [Wikipedia, Bhuvaneshvari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhuvaneshvari)

### 3.11 Bhairavī, Mātaṅgī, Kamalā / भैरवी, मातङ्गी, कमला
- Kāmākhyā and Rajrappa (above) are the documented shrines found so far. Wikipedia names **no** separate major temple for Bhairavī. For Mātaṅgī it mentions only a weakly sourced shrine in the Tulja Bhavani complex in Maharashtra. ⚠
- Sources: [Bhairavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhairavi) · [Matangi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matangi) · [Kamalatmika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamalatmika)

## 4. Images: rules before use / चित्र: प्रयोग से पहले नियम

The website should use **real photographs** only when their licence allows it.
1. Open the image's **Wikimedia Commons "File:" page** (not the category page) and record the author, the licence (CC BY‑SA 4.0, public domain, etc.) and the required attribution.
2. Many temples **forbid photographing the garbhagṛha (sanctum)**. Photos of the mūrti should come from the temple's own official release or from a licensed Commons file, never from an unattributed web copy.
3. Starting points: [Commons category: Dakshineswar Kali Temple](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Dakshineswar_Kali_Temple) (93 files) and [Commons category: Tripura Sundarī](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Tripura%20Sundar%C4%AB). For the other temples, use the infobox image of each Wikipedia article linked above.
