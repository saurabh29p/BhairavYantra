## सार (हिंदी में)

यह साइट एक **यंत्र के रूप में** बनाई जाएगी: बीच में बिन्दु (भैरव-तत्त्व), उसके चारों ओर त्रिकोण (तीन स्तंभ: देवता, ग्रंथ, परंपरा), फिर अष्टदल (अष्ट भैरव), दस दिशाओं में दश महाविद्याएँ, और बाहर भूपुर के चार द्वार (खोज, स्रोत-पद्धति, शब्दकोश, परिचय)। यंत्र **मुखपृष्ठ और मानचित्र** है; हर पृष्ठ सामान्य सूची और खोज से भी मिलेगा, ताकि मोबाइल पर और दृष्टिबाधित पाठकों के लिए भी साइट उपयोगी रहे।

साइट की सबसे बड़ी पहचान उसकी **सत्यनिष्ठा** होगी: हर कथन के साथ स्रोत और प्रमाण-श्रेणी (A/B/C/D/⚠)। ग्रंथों को पाँच स्पष्ट श्रेणियों में दिखाया जाएगा: (१) उपलब्ध, (२) खंडित, (३) लुप्त पर अन्य ग्रंथों में उद्धृत, (४) केवल नाम से ज्ञात, (५) "पुनः प्राप्त" होने का दावा। "लुप्त ग्रंथ पुनः प्राप्त" तभी लिखा जाएगा जब पांडुलिपि, उसका संग्रहालय/सूची-क्रमांक और विद्वानों का संस्करण दिखाया जा सके।

यह केवल विश्वकोश नहीं, एक **पूर्ण मंच** है: (१) **ग्रंथालय** (सत्यापित, संपादकों द्वारा नियंत्रित), (२) **संवादात्मक उपकरण** (ग्रंथ-पाठक, पाठभेद-तुलना, समय-रेखा, यंत्र-मानचित्र), और (३) **समुदाय** (खाते, चर्चा, साधकों का योगदान)। मूल सिद्धांत: समुदाय की बात तब तक ग्रंथालय में नहीं जाती जब तक संपादक उसका स्रोत जाँच न लें; समुदाय-सामग्री पर अलग चिह्न रहता है (§10)।

सबसे बड़े जोखिम: लुप्त ग्रंथों के बारे में बढ़ा-चढ़ाकर दावे, आधुनिक अनुवादों का कॉपीराइट, जीवित गुरुओं और परंपराओं के बारे में संवेदनशीलता, और मंत्रों का प्रदर्शन। इन सबके नियम नीचे दिए गए हैं।

## 1. The idea in one paragraph

The site is an encyclopedia whose **home page is a yantra**. Each layer of the yantra, from the bindu outward to the bhūpura, is a layer of the encyclopedia, so the structure of the diagram *is* the structure of the knowledge. What makes it different from every other Bhairava site is not the visuals but the **honesty layer**: every statement carries its source and an evidence grade, every text carries its survival status, and nothing is called "ancient", "secret" or "recovered" unless the evidence for that word is on the page.

It is a **one-stop platform with three parts that share one yantra**:

1. **Library / ग्रंथालय**: the verified encyclopedia and text collection. Editor-controlled, graded, cited.
2. **Interactive tools / संवादात्मक उपकरण**: the yantra map, a parallel-column text reader, variant comparison, timeline, glossary, personal study tools.
3. **Community / समुदाय**: accounts, discussions on every page, questions and answers, and a contribution pipeline through which sādhakas and scholars can bring sources, manuscripts, translations and corrections into the library.

The rule that holds the three together: **community content never becomes library content until an editor has checked its source**, and the two are always visibly distinct. The community *feeds* the library; it does not *write* it directly.

## 2. Critical review before we start / शुरू करने से पहले आलोचनात्मक समीक्षा

These are the points where the vision as stated could go wrong. Each one has a design answer later in this document.

### 2.1 "Bhairava yantra" is not one fixed diagram
There is no single canonical "Bhairava yantra". Different texts and lineages give different yantras for Baṭuka, Kāla, Svarṇākarṣaṇa and other Bhairavas, and printed popular yantras often have no traceable textual source. If the site draws a diagram and calls it *the* Bhairava yantra, that is exactly the kind of unsourced claim the research rules forbid.

**Answer:** call the home diagram a **yantra-form map** (*यंत्र-रूप मानचित्र*) and say on the page, in one line, that it is a navigational design built from the common elements of yantras (bindu, trikoṇa, aṣṭadala, vṛtta, bhūpura), not the ritual yantra of any specific text. Real yantras, with their textual sources and grades, are shown as content on the deity pages.

### 2.2 The geometry must not bend the facts
Some numbers fit the geometry beautifully (8 petals for the Aṣṭa Bhairavas; 10 directions for the Mahāvidyās). Others do not: lists vary, orders vary, and the Devī Bhāgavata 7.28 list is not a list of ten at all (see file 09). The risk is that the diagram quietly presents one variant as the only one.

**Answer:** the yantra shows **the most widely attested variant, labelled as such**, and every position links to a page where all variants appear side by side. The diagram never carries a claim the deity page does not.

### 2.3 "Ancient to modern, lost and recovered" is the highest-risk promise
This is where most sites in this field fail. Many works circulate as "from the Rudrayāmala" or "a lost tantra recovered by guru X" with no manuscript evidence. If the site repeats such claims, it loses the one thing that can make it valuable: trust.

**Answer:** the text-status framework in §5. "Recovered" becomes a word with a definition and an evidence checklist.

### 2.4 Radial navigation is beautiful and hard to use
A circular diagram works on a large screen. On a phone, eight petals and ten directions become tiny tap targets. Screen-reader users cannot use a picture as navigation. Search engines index lists better than SVG.

**Answer:** the yantra is the home page and an optional map. Every page is also reachable from a plain hierarchical menu, from search, and from an A–Z index. On phones, the yantra becomes **concentric layers you step through** (tap the bindu, the next ring opens) rather than a single dense picture.

### 2.5 Sacredness and an encyclopedia pull in different directions
A sacred site invites devotion; an encyclopedia invites checking. Devotional design (glowing deities, chanting audio, "benefits of this mantra") would undermine the evidence-first voice. Pure academic design would feel cold to the sādhakas the site also serves.

**Answer:** the tone is **reverent and restrained**: respectful visual language, Devanāgarī given first place, no sensationalism, no promises of results, no commerce. The content voice stays documentary ("the Kūrma Purāṇa says…"), never prescriptive ("chant this to…").

### 2.6 Scope is enormous
Bhairava alone has 8 principal and 64 subordinate forms in popular lists, plus the Bhairava Tantras, Kashmir Śaivism, Kāpālika and Nātha material, temples and sādhakas. A site that launches only when "complete" will never launch.

**Answer:** publish in rings (§12). A ring goes live when its pages meet the minimum evidence standard, and unfinished pages say so openly ("Draft: primary source not yet located"), as file 02 already does.

### 2.7 A community changes what the project is
A static encyclopedia needs editors. A community needs **moderators, every day, indefinitely**. In this particular field a community also attracts predictable problems: people selling "tantra services", vaśīkaraṇa and black-magic offers, self-declared gurus recruiting followers, lineage quarrels, harassment of women practitioners, and confident unsourced claims that drown out careful ones. It also brings legal duties (user accounts mean personal data; hosting user posts means grievance handling under Indian intermediary rules). None of this is a reason not to do it, but it is the single biggest ongoing cost of the whole project.

**Answer:** §10. Keep the verified library and the community structurally separate; give members real work (finding sources, reporting manuscripts, reviewing translations) so that standing comes from verified contributions, not from spiritual claims; use a mature forum engine rather than building one; open the community **after** the library has enough content to discuss, with moderators in place before the doors open.

## 3. The yantra-form map: what lives where / यंत्र-रूप मानचित्र: कौन-सी सामग्री कहाँ

Reading from the centre outward mirrors the traditional movement from the unmanifest centre to the manifest world, and also the movement from **principle** to **detail** that a reader needs.

| Layer | Sanskrit / हिंदी | Content placed here | Why this fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bindu** | बिन्दु | **Bhairava** as principle: who Bhairava is, the origin accounts (file 01), the meaning of the name as the texts explain it. Also the entry to the site's purpose statement. | The single point from which everything else unfolds. |
| **Trikoṇa** | त्रिकोण | **The three pillars** of the encyclopedia: **Devatā** (deities and forms) · **Grantha** (texts) · **Paramparā** (traditions, sādhakas, places). | Three gateways into everything below; a reader chooses by *what* they are looking for. |
| **Ṣaṭkoṇa** *(optional)* | षट्कोण | **Śiva–Śakti**: the Bhairava–Bhairavī pairings, including the consort Bhairava of each Mahāvidyā once file 10 verifies them. | The interlaced triangles are commonly read as the union of Śiva and Śakti. Include only if this ring has enough verified content; otherwise leave it out. |
| **Aṣṭadala** (8 petals) | अष्टदल | **Aṣṭa Bhairavas**: Asitāṅga, Ruru, Caṇḍa, Krodha, Unmatta, Kapālī, Bhīṣaṇa, Saṃhāra. Each petal opens to its 8 subordinate forms (the 64). | Eight petals for eight forms. File 02 marks the list's primary source as not yet located; the petal pages must carry that status. |
| **Ten directions** (8 compass points + zenith + nadir) | दश दिशाएँ | **Daśa Mahāvidyās**, placed by the direction the Satī episode assigns them, *once verified* in the Mahābhāgavata ch. 8 / Bṛhaddharma Madhya 6 (file 09 lists these as ⚠). Until then, placed in the Kinsley order, labelled. | The episode itself has the ten forms surround Śiva in the ten directions, so the geometry comes from the story rather than being imposed on it. |
| **Vṛtta** (enclosing circles) | वृत्त | **Bhairavīs and the wider Śakti world**: Bhairavī as Mahāvidyā, the consorts of the Aṣṭa Bhairavas (Aṣṭa Mātṛkās where texts pair them), Yoginīs. | The circle that holds and encloses the inner forms. |
| **Bhūpura** (square with four gates) | भूपुर | The **four gates** of the outer world of the site: **(1) Search & A–Z index**, **(2) Sources & Method** (evidence grades, editions, claims register), **(3) Glossary** (Sanskrit terms in Devanāgarī + IAST + Hindi + English), **(4) About & Ethics** (purpose, mantra policy, corrections, contact). | The bhūpura is where one enters and leaves; it holds the practical machinery. |

**Placement of Grantha and Paramparā:** these do not fit on the petals and should not be forced. They are the two other corners of the trikoṇa and open into their own **sub-yantras**, simpler diagrams with the same visual language:

- **Grantha sub-map:** concentric rings by **text status** (§5): extant at the centre, then fragmentary, then lost-but-quoted, then known-by-name, with claimed-recovered texts shown *outside* the bhūpura, in their own clearly marked zone until evidence moves them in. This makes the honesty visible at a glance.
- **Paramparā sub-map:** by tradition (Kāpālika, Kālāmukha, Krama, Trika/Kashmir Śaiva, Nātha, Kaula, Aghora, regional temple traditions) and by place (Kāśī, Ujjain, the Aṣṭa Bhairava temples, Kathmandu valley, etc.).

## 4. Content model / सामग्री का ढाँचा

Every page is one of these types. Fields marked **required** must be present for the page to build (enforced by the schema, §11).

### 4.1 Devatā (deity or form)
- `id`, names: **Devanāgarī** (required), **IAST** (required), common Hindi and English spellings (for search)
- `type`: Bhairava form / Bhairavī / Mahāvidyā / Mātṛkā / Yoginī / other
- `yantra_position`: which layer and slot, plus the variant this placement follows
- `summary_en`, `summary_hi` (required, 2–4 sentences each)
- `textual_sources[]`: list of **Citation** objects (required, at least one, even if grade D)
- `name_variants[]`: alternative names, each with its source
- `iconography`: dhyāna verse(s) as **Passage** objects; attributes (colour, vehicle, weapons) as a **variant table**, never a single value unless only one source exists
- `consorts[]`, `related[]`: links to other Devatā with the source for the relation
- `mantras[]`: **Mantra** objects (§4.4)
- `yantras[]`: diagrams with source
- `places[]`: temples/kṣetras
- `status`: draft / reviewed / stable, plus `last_reviewed` date
- `open_questions[]`: what is not yet verified (shown on the page, not hidden)

### 4.2 Grantha (text)
- names (Devanāgarī, IAST), `genre` (Purāṇa, Tantra/Āgama, Yāmala, stotra, nibandha/digest, commentary, modern)
- `text_status`: one of the five statuses in §5 (required), with `status_evidence[]` (required)
- `date_range` with grade (dating is almost always grade C, and must say so)
- `editions[]`: **Edition** objects (editor, publisher, year, series, and online location if any)
- `manuscripts[]`: repository, catalogue/accession number, date if known, script, completeness
- `quoted_in[]`: for lost texts, every later text that quotes or names it, with location
- `contents_summary`, `related_devata[]`
- `copyright_note` for every edition or translation we display (§7)

### 4.3 Passage (a quoted verse or prose unit)
- `text_sa` (Devanāgarī, verbatim from the named edition, required)
- `iast` (optional), `translation_en`, `translation_hi`, each with `translator` ("ours" or a named person) and licence
- `location`: text, edition, chapter, verse (required)
- `grade` (required)
- `variant_readings[]`

### 4.4 Mantra
- `text_sa` (required, verbatim), with `viniyoga`, `ṛṣi`, `chandas`, `devatā`, `bīja`, `śakti` *if and only if the source gives them*
- `source_text` + location (required); `where_we_read_it` link (required, per the methodology's rule 3)
- `attribution_check`: if the mantra claims to come from text X but the edition of X we have does not contain it, say so (e.g., "Claimed: Rudrayāmala. Not found in [edition]. Grade D ⚠.")
- `tradition_note`: which lineages use it, if sourced
- **Never** a "benefits" field. If a source text states a phalaśruti (fruits of recitation), it is quoted as a Passage, attributed to the text, not presented in the site's own voice.

### 4.5 Sādhaka / Ācārya (person)
- names, dates (with grade), tradition, works authored (links to Grantha)
- `historicity`: historical (documented) / traditional (hagiographic) / mixed, with sources for each part
- **Living persons:** see §8.3; default is *not included*.

### 4.6 Place (kṣetra / temple)
- name, location, presiding form, textual mentions (with citations) kept separate from temple lore (grade D)

### 4.7 Claim (the claims register)
- `statement`, `who_claims_it` (site, book, tradition), `status` (unverified / disputed / refuted / verified), `evidence_for[]`, `evidence_against[]`, `pages_affected[]`
- Public page: "Claims under review / दावों की समीक्षा". This is one of the site's strongest features: it shows readers the checking in progress.

### 4.7A Image
- `file` (stored in the repo, optimised), `alt_en`, `alt_hi` (required)
- `depicts[]`: Devatā ids, plus `identified_by` (source / ours) and `identification_note`
- `object_type`, `material`, `origin_place`, `period` + `period_grade` (required)
- `current_location` (temple or museum) and `accession_number` where applicable
- `source_url`, `creator`, `retrieved_on` (required)
- `licence`, `licence_url`, `attribution_text` (required; build fails without them)
- `in_worship`, `inner_shrine`, `temple_permission` (required flags)
- `permission_file` (required when the licence is "permission")

### 4.8 Citation and Edition
Every citation resolves to an Edition entry in a single bibliography file, so an edition is described once and cited everywhere. Displayed as a short form inline ("KūP Uttara 31.30 · A‑w") that expands to full details and a clickable link.

## 5. Texts across time: an honest status framework / ग्रंथों की स्थिति का ईमानदार ढाँचा

This is the core of "ancient to modern, lost and recovered". Every Grantha page shows one status badge, with its evidence beneath.

| Status | हिंदी | Definition | Minimum evidence to use this label |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Extant** | उपलब्ध | The text survives as a whole (or near whole) in manuscripts and/or print. Sub-labels: *critical edition* / *printed (vulgate)* / *manuscript only, unedited*. | At least one named edition or a manuscript with repository and catalogue number. |
| **2. Fragmentary** | खंडित | Survives only in part: damaged manuscripts, incomplete chapters. | Which parts survive, from which manuscripts. |
| **3. Lost, known through quotation** | लुप्त, उद्धरणों से ज्ञात | No manuscript known, but later works quote passages from it. | Each quoting text with chapter/verse, and the quoted passage. |
| **4. Lost, known by name only** | लुप्त, केवल नाम से ज्ञात | Named in lists or references, no surviving passages identified. | The source of the name (e.g., a canonical list of tantras), with location. |
| **5. Claimed recovered / claimed source** | पुनः प्राप्त होने का दावा | Someone states that a lost text has been found, or a work claims to belong to a famous text, but this has not been independently established. | The claim and its source. Shown in a separate zone, visually outside the main map. |

**Moving a text from status 5 to status 1 or 2** requires all of: (a) a named manuscript with repository and catalogue/accession number; (b) a description of it (script, date or estimated date, extent); (c) an edition or at least a published description by a named scholar; (d) a reason to identify it with the lost text (e.g., it contains passages that earlier works quote from that text). Without all four, it stays at 5, however respected the claimant.

**Language rules that follow from this:**
- Use "lost" only with status 3 or 4, "recovered" only when a text has moved from 3/4 to 1/2 by the checklist above, and "ancient" only with a date range and its grade.
- "Rare" means hard to obtain (out of print, unedited manuscript), not "secret" or "suppressed". The site does not use the words "secret", "forbidden" or "hidden knowledge" in its own voice.
- Many stotras and kavacas say they come from the Rudrayāmala, Dāmara Tantra, etc. That attribution is recorded as a *claim*, checked against an edition where one exists, and labelled with the result.

**Where real recovery has happened**, the site can tell those stories well and truthfully. Modern scholarship has identified and edited early Śaiva texts from manuscripts preserved in Nepal and elsewhere, through cataloguing efforts and institutions such as the Nepal-German Manuscript Preservation Project, the Institut Français de Pondichéry and the Muktabodha digital library; the edition of the Brahmayāmala by Shaman Hatley and Csaba Kiss, already listed in our methodology file, is one example. *(These institutional details are grade C from general scholarly knowledge and must be confirmed with citations before publication.)* A "Recovered texts" page built only from such documented cases will be more impressive than any unverified claim, because readers can follow the evidence.

**Grantha timeline view:** a horizontal timeline from the Purāṇic accounts through the Bhairava Tantras, the Kashmir Śaiva exegetes, medieval digests, to modern editions and translations. Every date is shown as a **range bar**, not a point, with its grade, because nearly all dates in this field are scholarly estimates.

## 6. Accuracy and citation standard on the site / साइट पर स्रोत और सटीकता का मानक

The research methodology carries over unchanged. On the website it becomes visible like this:

1. **Inline grade badges.** Every factual sentence or table row that makes a claim ends with a compact citation + grade, e.g. `KūP Utt. 31.30 · A‑w`. Hover/tap shows the full edition and link.
2. **Grade legend** on every page footer, linking to Sources & Method.
3. **Variant tables** wherever sources disagree, with the column header naming each source.
4. **"Our rendering" label** on every English/Hindi translation we made, so it is never mistaken for a published translation.
5. **Page status banner** for draft pages, including what is missing (as file 02 does now).
6. **Change history.** Each page shows "last reviewed" and links to its revision history (Git history is the audit trail). Corrections are logged on a public corrections page.
7. **Corrections channel.** A visible "Suggest a correction (with source)" link. Reports without a source go to a queue; they do not change content directly.
8. **No AI-written content without verification.** Drafts made with AI assistance (including Claude) are only published after the citations are checked against the source. The build should fail if a page has a claim with no citation (§11).

## 7. Copyright and permissions / कॉपीराइट और अनुमतियाँ

This is a real legal risk, not just courtesy.

- **Sanskrit source texts** of old works are public domain. **Modern critical editions** can still carry rights in their editorial work (apparatus, introductions, and arguably the constituted text in some jurisdictions). Quoting verses with citation is normal scholarly practice; reproducing a whole edition is not safe.
- **Modern translations are copyrighted.** This includes the Motilal Banarsidass Purāṇa translations, Gita Press Hindi translations, Kinsley, Hatley, and most material on Wisdom Library. Policy: **use our own translations**; quote published translations only in short excerpts with full credit when the exact wording matters; link to the source rather than copying it.
- **Images.** Real images of the deities are a core part of the site (see §7A). Temple and deity photographs belong to their photographers or temples, and some temples forbid photography of the inner shrine. Use only images with a clear licence (own photos, public domain, Creative Commons with attribution, museum open access, or written permission), and record the licence in each image's metadata.
- **AI-generated deity images.** Recommendation: **do not use them** for deities. They often get iconography wrong (wrong number of arms, attributes, vehicle), which contradicts the site's accuracy standard, and many devotees consider them inappropriate. Use traditional sources (public-domain paintings, temple images with permission) or clean line-drawn iconographic diagrams that are explicitly based on a cited dhyāna verse.
- **Old public-domain books** (e.g., Gopinatha Rao, 1916) can be quoted and their plates reproduced, which is valuable for iconography.
- The site's own text: choose a licence (e.g., CC BY-SA 4.0 for our writing) so others can reuse it with credit. Decide this before launch.

## 7A. Real images of the deities / देवताओं के वास्तविक चित्र

Every deity page should show **real, historical images**: temple mūrtis, stone and bronze sculptures, manuscript paintings, pāṭa and thangka paintings, and plates from old iconography books. Treated as evidence, they are one of the strongest parts of the site, because they show how each form was actually depicted, in which place and century.

### Which images qualify
| Kind | Examples of where to find them | Licence situation |
|---|---|---|
| Museum objects (sculpture, bronze, painting) | Museum open-access collections, e.g. The Metropolitan Museum of Art (Open Access, CC0), the Cleveland Museum of Art (Open Access, CC0), Rijksmuseum; the Los Angeles County Museum of Art and the Art Institute of Chicago publish many public-domain images | Usually public domain or CC0; check each object page |
| Wikimedia Commons photographs | Temple sculptures, Kathmandu valley Bhairavas, Aṣṭa Bhairava temples, museum photos | Each file has its own licence (often CC BY-SA, which requires attribution and share-alike); some files are wrongly licensed, so check the uploader and source |
| Plates from old books | T. A. Gopinatha Rao, *Elements of Hindu Iconography* (1914–16); other pre-1929 publications | Public domain |
| Manuscript illustrations | Digitised collections of libraries and museums | Depends on the holding institution; many are public domain, some restrict reuse |
| Your own photographs | Temple visits | Yours, **but** only where the temple allows photography; get permission for the garbhagṛha (inner shrine) |
| Photographs by others | Photographers, temples, scholars | Only with written permission, stored in the repo |

*(Museum licence details are general knowledge, grade C; confirm the licence on each object page before use.)*

**Not allowed:** AI-generated or AI-"enhanced" deity images, images copied from Google results or social media, calendar/poster art still under copyright, and watermarked stock photos.

### What every image must record
An image without a source is treated like a claim without a source: the build rejects it. Required fields (see the **Image** type in §4):
- **What it shows:** deity/form as identified **by the holding institution or source**, and our identification if different (with the reason, e.g. matching attributes to a dhyāna verse).
- **Object facts:** type (mūrti, relief, bronze, painting, manuscript folio, book plate, photograph), material, **place of origin**, **period/date** (with grade, since most dates are estimates), current location (temple or museum, with accession number).
- **Source:** URL of the source page, photographer/creator, and the date we downloaded it.
- **Licence:** exact licence name and link, and the attribution text to display.
- **Sensitivity flags:** in active worship (yes/no), inner-shrine photo (yes/no), temple permission (yes/no/not needed).

### How images are shown
- Caption under every image in English and Hindi: what it is, where it is, its period, and the credit line.
- An "Image details" link opens the full record (source, licence, identification evidence).
- A **gallery per deity**, ordered by period, so readers can see how the form developed. Where identification is uncertain, the caption says so ("identified by the museum as Bhairava; attributes match Baṭuka").
- Images are respectful: no cropping that cuts the deity's head or feet, no filters or colour "enhancement", no use as decorative backgrounds.

### Choices only you can make
1. **Photos of consecrated mūrtis in worship.** Many temples and devotees hold that a consecrated mūrti should not be photographed or shown out of context. Recommendation: use them only where the temple allows photography, and prefer museum objects and book plates for the rest.
2. **Hero image for each deity page:** a real object (recommended), or a clean line drawing based on the dhyāna verse.
3. **Your own temple photography:** whether you (or others) will photograph temples for the site, which would need a simple permission form.
4. **Share-alike licences:** CC BY-SA images are fine, but share-alike applies to the image (and edits of it). It fits with the CC BY-SA licence recommended for the site's own text (§13).

## 8. Sacred, respectful design and ethics / पवित्रता, सम्मान और नैतिकता

### 8.1 Visual and tonal principles
- **Devanāgarī first.** Sanskrit appears in Devanāgarī in a proper Sanskrit-capable typeface (e.g., Tiro Devanagari Sanskrit or Noto Serif Devanagari), large enough to read conjuncts clearly, with IAST beneath.
- **Restrained palette.** Deep night blue-black (Bhairava as Kāla), ash grey (bhasma), a single vermilion/sindūra accent, and gold used sparingly for the yantra lines. Light and dark modes both designed properly, not inverted.
- **No sensationalism.** No skulls-and-smoke stock imagery, no horror aesthetics, no "dark tantra" framing. The fierce (ugra) forms are presented as the texts present them, which is serious, not lurid.
- **Calm motion.** The yantra may draw itself slowly on first load; respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. No autoplay audio, ever.
- **No ads, no affiliate links, no shop.** Selling "energised yantras" or pūjā services on an encyclopedia page would destroy the trust it is built on. If funding is needed later, use a simple donation page kept separate from content.

### 8.2 Mantra display ethics / मंत्र प्रदर्शन
Your standing preference is to give mantras in full with clickable sources, and the research files already do so. The concept keeps that. To make it respectful as well as complete:

- Each mantra block opens with a short fixed note: *"Recorded from [text, location]. Traditionally, mantras are received through dīkṣā from a qualified guru; this page documents what the text says and is not a substitute for that."* (EN + HI). The texts themselves say this, so it is a citation, not a disclaimer we invented.
- Show the text **exactly as the source gives it**, including its viniyoga, and mark any difference between editions.
- **No audio pronunciation, no "how to chant", no counts promised to bring results, no "benefits" in the site's voice.** If the source text has a phalaśruti, quote it as the text's statement.
- **Design option for you to decide (see §13):** show mantras open on the page (simplest, matches your preference), or show them behind a single "Show the text" tap. The tap pattern signals respect without hiding anything and keeps mantras out of search-result snippets. Recommendation: **open on the page**, with the note above; only switch if traditional readers raise it.

### 8.3 Guru-paramparā and living traditions / गुरु-परंपरा
- **Do not certify or rank gurus, lineages or practitioners.** The site documents; it does not endorse.
- **Living persons are excluded by default.** Including a living teacher brings consent, defamation and impersonation risks, and invites lineage disputes. Exception only with the person's written consent, and limited to publicly documented facts.
- **Historical ācāryas** (e.g., Abhinavagupta, Kṣemarāja, Gorakhnāth): separate documented history (grade A–C) from hagiography (grade D), both shown, neither mocked.
- **No contact directory of "tantrikas".** Such listings attract fraud and exploitation, a known problem in this field. If readers ask where to learn, point them to established institutions and temples, not individuals.
- **Competing lineage claims** (who is the true holder of a paramparā, which version of a ritual is correct) are recorded as variants with their sources, never settled by the site.
- **Sensitive practices** (Kaula pañcamakāra, cremation-ground practice, historical animal offering, Aghora) are described accurately from the texts and from scholarship, without either sensationalising or sanitising them, and without instructions.
- **Respect for regional and caste-diverse traditions.** Many Bhairava traditions are village, temple and community traditions with no Sanskrit text at all. They are grade D by our scale, and that must not read as "lesser": label D as "living tradition / जीवित परंपरा", not as "unreliable".

## 9. Bilingual handling (Hindi + English) / द्विभाषी व्यवस्था

**Recommendation: one page per topic, both languages on it, in parallel**, which is how the research files already work and what you prefer. A language toggle sets which language comes first and which the interface (menus, buttons) uses; it does not hide the other.

- **Three scripts, clear roles:** Sanskrit in Devanāgarī (primary) + IAST; Hindi in Devanāgarī; English in Latin script. Sanskrit and Hindi must be visually distinguishable (different weight or a small "सं." / "हि." label), because both use Devanāgarī and readers must know which is source and which is translation.
- **URLs** in readable Latin script: `/devata/kala-bhairava`, `/grantha/svacchanda-tantra`. Each page also has its Devanāgarī title in the `<title>` and headings so Hindi searchers find it.
- **Search across spellings.** "Bhairav", "Bhairava", "bhairav", "भैरव", "Bhairo" must all find the same page. Implement with a synonyms list per entity plus IAST-to-plain-ASCII folding (ā→a, ś/ṣ→s/sh, etc.).
- **Hindi is not a machine translation.** Every Hindi passage is written or reviewed by a fluent reader. Mark any unreviewed Hindi as such.
- **Numbers:** verse numbers in Devanāgarī numerals in the Hindi column (३१.३०) and Arabic numerals in the English column, as the research files do.
- **Typography:** load only the Devanāgarī and Latin subsets needed; test conjuncts (क्ष, ज्ञ, श्र, ह्म, द्ध, ङ्ग) and the candrabindu/anusvāra used in bīja syllables, which many web fonts render badly.

## 10. Library, interaction and community / ग्रंथालय, संवाद और समुदाय

### 10.1 Where the three parts sit on the yantra
The yantra stays the single map. The **library** is everything inside the bhūpura (§3). The **interactive tools** are how you move through it. The **community** gets a **fifth gate**, *Sabhā / सभा* (assembly), plus a discussion panel at the foot of every library page. Community content always sits visually *outside* the library content it discusses, with a different background and a "Community / समुदाय" label, so a reader can never mistake a member's post for a verified entry.

*(A square has four gates; a fifth "gate" breaks the geometry. Alternative: make Sabhā the space around the bhūpura, i.e. the world outside the yantra where people gather. That is truer to the form; the designer can choose.)*

### 10.2 Library features / ग्रंथालय
- **Text reader** for each Grantha: parallel columns (Sanskrit Devanāgarī · IAST · Hindi · English), verse by verse, variant readings toggleable, and a permanent link to every verse (`/grantha/kurma-purana/uttara/31/30`).
- **Edition shelf**: for each text, every known edition and manuscript, linking to legitimate digital copies (archive.org, Muktabodha, GRETIL, library catalogues) rather than re-hosting copyrighted scans.
- **Rare-book catalogue**: out-of-print and hard-to-find works, with where a reader can actually access them (library, digital archive, reprint). Not a download site for copyrighted books.
- **Reading paths**: "Start here" sequences for beginners, scholars and sādhakas, built from library pages.

### 10.3 Interactive tools / संवादात्मक उपकरण
- **Yantra map**: explore by layer; filter by text or tradition, which lights up the slots that text discusses.
- **Variant comparison**: pick a topic (e.g., the Aṣṭa Bhairava list or the Mahāvidyā order) and see every source's version side by side, differences highlighted.
- **Grantha timeline** with date ranges and grades (§5).
- **Glossary pop-ups** on any Sanskrit term, in four forms (Devanāgarī, IAST, Hindi, English).
- **Personal study tools** (with an account): bookmarks, highlights, private notes on any verse, reading lists, "continue reading". Private by default.
- **Sacred calendar** (later ring): Bhairava Aṣṭamī, Mahāvidyā jayantīs etc. Dates depend on pañcāṅga calculation and regional conventions, so each date must name its method and source. Build only once a reliable source is chosen.

### 10.4 Community features / समुदाय
| Feature | Purpose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Accounts and profiles** | Identity, study tools, participation | Display name, languages, interests, tradition (optional, self-declared, never verified by the site). **No "guru", "siddha" or initiation badges.** |
| **Page discussions** | Discuss a deity, text or verse | Threads attached to each library page, shown collapsed beneath it. |
| **Sabhā forum** | Open discussion by topic | Categories mirror the yantra: Bhairava, Aṣṭa Bhairava, Mahāvidyās, Granthas, Traditions, Places, Site feedback. Hindi and English welcome everywhere. |
| **Questions and answers** | Readers ask, knowledgeable members answer | Answers with sources are visibly marked; editors can promote a sourced answer into a library correction. |
| **Source hunts / स्रोत-खोज** | Turn open questions into community tasks | Every ⚠ item in the claims register and every "primary source not yet located" becomes a public task, e.g., "Find the Aṣṭa Bhairava listing verse in a primary text". This is the community's most valuable work. |
| **Manuscript and edition reports** | Members report a manuscript, old print or temple text they know of | Structured form: repository, catalogue number, photos (with permission), description. Feeds §5 status reviews. |
| **Translation and proofreading** | Help with Hindi/English renderings and Devanāgarī proofing | Credited by name on the page; an editor approves before publication. |
| **Temple and tradition notes** | Living traditions with no text | Recorded as grade D "living tradition", with contributor, place and date, and the temple's consent where needed. |
| **Study circles** (later) | Small groups reading a text together | Public or invite-only, tied to the text reader. |

**Deliberately left out:** private messages between strangers at launch (the main channel for fraud, solicitation and harassment in religious communities; add later only for trusted members, with reporting), marketplaces, paid consultations, "ask a tantrik" services, horoscope or remedy offers, and anything that lets one member give another ritual prescriptions.

### 10.5 How a contribution becomes library content / योगदान से ग्रंथालय तक
1. A member submits a correction, source, manuscript report or translation through a structured form that **requires** a source: text, edition, location, link or photo.
2. It enters a public **review queue**: submitted → under review → accepted / needs more evidence / declined (with a reason).
3. A **reviewer** checks the source itself, not the contributor's reputation, and assigns the grade using the methodology scale.
4. An **editor** merges it into the library. The change appears in the page history and corrections log, credited to the contributor.
5. Unsourced contributions are not thrown away; they become a **source hunt**.

### 10.6 Roles and trust / भूमिकाएँ
| Role | Can do | How earned |
|---|---|---|
| Reader | Read everything; no account needed | — |
| Member | Discuss, ask, bookmark, submit contributions | Sign up, accept community rules, **18+** |
| Contributor | Higher posting limits, edit own submissions in queue | A number of accepted contributions |
| Reviewer | Check sources in the queue, assign grades | Appointed by editors for demonstrated source work, preferably with Sanskrit reading ability |
| Moderator | Enforce community rules | Appointed; at least one Hindi-fluent moderator from launch |
| Editor | Merge into library; final say on content | You, plus people you appoint |

Standing comes **only from verified contributions**, never from claimed initiation, lineage or attainment. That one rule prevents most of the status games this field is prone to.

### 10.7 Community rules (draft) / समुदाय के नियम
- Cite sources for factual claims; say "in my tradition…" for lineage teaching.
- No selling or advertising services, yantras, rituals, remedies or consultations. No vaśīkaraṇa, māraṇa, "black magic" or curse offers or requests.
- No soliciting followers, initiation or money. No claiming to give dīkṣā online.
- No ritual prescriptions to other members, and no medical, psychological or legal advice dressed as sādhanā.
- Respect every sampradāya: disagree with sources, not with insults. No casteist, sexist or communal content.
- No sharing of copyrighted books or translations; link to legitimate sources.
- Report, don't retaliate.

### 10.8 Legal and safety basics (to confirm with a professional)
- **Personal data:** accounts bring obligations under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023. Collect the minimum (email, display name), publish a privacy notice, allow account deletion. *(General understanding, grade C; take advice before launch.)*
- **User content:** hosting user posts generally brings duties under India's IT (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021, including a published grievance mechanism and timely handling of complaints. *(Grade C; confirm.)*
- **Age:** members 18+, given the subject matter; this also avoids the stricter consent rules for children's data.
- **Moderation capacity:** do not open the community until at least two moderators (one Hindi-fluent) have agreed to cover it.

### 10.9 Technology consequence
A community cannot be a purely static site. Recommended split:
- **Library + tools** stay static (Astro): fast, cheap, as in §11.
- **Accounts, study tools, contributions queue**: a managed backend, **Supabase** (Postgres, authentication, row-level security, file storage for manuscript photos), called from Astro.
- **Forum and page discussions**: **Discourse** (open source; self-hosted or paid hosting) instead of a custom forum. It already has trust levels, flagging, moderation queues, rate limits, a Hindi interface, single sign-on, and an embed for per-page discussions. Building these from scratch is months of work and where most safety failures come from.
- **Single sign-on** between the site account and Discourse, so a member has one identity.

Chosen for: content-heavy, rarely changing, must be fast in India on mobile data, must keep a full edit history, and must be maintainable by you with Claude Code.

| Need | Recommendation | Why |
|---|---|---|

| Hosting | **Cloudflare Pages** or **Netlify** (free tier) or GitHub Pages | Free, fast CDN with Indian edge locations, deploys automatically from the repo. |

| Analytics (optional) | A privacy-respecting option (e.g., Plausible or Cloudflare Web Analytics), or none | No tracking of readers of sacred material. |

**Not recommended:** a WordPress-style CMS (weak schema enforcement, plugin security burden), a hand-built forum or authentication system, or any client-side heavy framework for the yantra.

## 15. Risks register / जोखिम सूची

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overclaiming lost/recovered texts | High | Destroys credibility | §5 status framework and checklist; claims register |
| Copyright complaint over translations or images | Medium | Takedowns, legal cost | Own translations; licence metadata per image; short quotes only |
| Diagram presents one variant as the truth | High | Silent inaccuracy | Variant label on the map; full variants on every page |
| AI-assisted drafting introduces fabricated citations | Medium | Silent inaccuracy | Rule 1 of the brief; build fails on uncited claims; human check before publish |
| Lineage disputes / offence to a sampradāya | Medium | Reputation, conflict | Document, don't adjudicate; no living persons by default; corrections channel |
| Misuse as a ritual manual, or by people selling "tantra services" | Medium | Harm to readers | Documentary voice; no instructions, no directory, no commerce |
| Scope never finishes | High | Never launches | Ring-by-ring publishing with honest draft banners |
| Poor Devanāgarī rendering on some phones | Medium | Unreadable mantras | Font testing in CI; self-hosted, well-tested fonts |
| Community overrun by service-sellers, self-styled gurus or harassment | High without moderation | Severe: drives away serious members | Rules in §10.7; Discourse trust levels; no DMs at launch; moderators before opening |
| Community claims leak into the library unverified | Medium | Silent inaccuracy | Contribution pipeline §10.5; visual separation of community content |
| Personal-data or intermediary-rule non-compliance | Medium | Legal | Minimal data, privacy notice, grievance contact, professional advice (§10.8) |
| Radial navigation unusable on mobile / for screen readers | High | Lost readers | Stepped mobile layers; plain nav and index everywhere |
